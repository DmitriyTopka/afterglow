# Eval v2 on the real catalog (06.10.2026)

- 40 shopper messages and labels written by a blind subagent that saw only the catalog (id, title, category, price);
  frozen in commit a64b2cb before any run. Groups: 14 tastes in the store, 16 tastes not in the store, 10 multi-format.
- Signals extracted once with Claude Haiku ($0.034) and reused by every arm. 414 live Qloo calls.
- Arms: rules (store metadata only: titles by or of a named taste, then titles sharing a creator; no Qloo, no tags),
  qloo (Qloo lift only), hybrid (the live agent: direct matches, then Qloo lift). Same budget rule and
  one-per-category shortlist for all.
- Pooled: every unlabelled pick from any arm judged yes/no by a second blind subagent (19 yes / 189 no).

Reading
- Without hand-written tags, Qloo clearly beats store metadata: qloo vs rules 10 wins / 2 losses; where the named
  taste is not stocked, rules have nothing to offer and Qloo finds 0.15.
- Absolute precision is low. Two causes seen in the rows: (1) the hybrid puts the exact title the shopper already
  loves first ("I loved Dune: Part Two" -> Dune: Part Two), which labels treat as owned; (2) for same-format asks
  (Hunger Games -> Divergent) the lift ranks cross-format titles above obvious same-format ones.
- A diagnostic without the one-per-category rule and without named titles moves hybrid only to 0.17, so cause (2)
  dominates.
- Next: any ranking change is chosen on the odd-numbered scenarios and confirmed once on the even-numbered ones.
